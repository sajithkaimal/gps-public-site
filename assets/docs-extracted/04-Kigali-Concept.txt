Kigali Knowledge Crossroads Week (October 2027)
Kigali Knowledge Crossroads Week (October 2027) will be built around the GCUB General Assembly, an international conference, thematic workshops, and a collective plan of action. Its workshops are structured around a continuous knowledge cycle—from vision and plural knowledge systems to learning, innovation, and societal solutions—ensuring that knowledge remains in motion and responsive to real-world challenges. 
Background
As part of its annual General Assembly, GCUB and its member institutions will gather in Kigali in October 2027, bringing together university leaders and partners from across the Global South and beyond. In parallel, GPS will convene Kigali Knowledge Crossroads Week, its flagship global gathering. This convening will bring together universities, policymakers, public institutions, entrepreneurs, artists, and civil society actors from five continents. 
The Week is intentionally designed not as a traditional academic conference, but as an action-oriented platform where:
research informs policy 
education responds to societal needs 
ideas become actionable solutions 
partnerships generate measurable social and economic impact 
Rationale
At a time when education systems, labor markets, emerging technologies—especially artificial intelligence—and global power relations are being reshaped, Kigali Knowledge Crossroads responds to the urgent need to realign learning, research, and public action. 
Across the Global South, universities produce critical knowledge in education, public health, digital transformation, climate resilience, governance, and entrepreneurship. Yet too often this knowledge remains confined within academic spaces, disconnected from policymaking, economic transformation, and everyday realities. 
At the same time:
governments seek evidence-based and context-sensitive policies 
education systems must rethink curricula, pedagogies, and methodologies 
the private sector requires research-informed innovation and graduates aligned with real-world needs 
Education must be in motion: responsive to lived realities, grounded in local and global knowledge, and equipped with adaptive curricula and methodologies. Research must be in action: translated into policy, entrepreneurship, and social impact. Knowledge, therefore, should function as a social contract linking universities to society, connecting research to decision-making, and aligning education with community aspirations. 
Vision
Kigali Knowledge Crossroads Week is conceived as a catalytic moment defined by its integrative and cyclical architecture—bringing together universities, governments, and innovators within a sustained transcontinental process where knowledge is co-produced, shared, and mobilized for social and human development. 
It advances an approach to knowledge that is context-responsive, historically grounded, and socially embedded—engaging lived experience while contributing to global conversations on development, governance, and innovation. Strategically, the Crossroads operates as a collaborative platform where universities, public institutions, and enterprises co-design practical and scalable solutions for sustainable development across the Global South. 
Objectives
The Week aims to:
Advance educational reconfiguration through curriculum innovation and adaptive pedagogies 
Strengthen dialogue between universities and public institutions for evidence-based policymaking 
Catalyze university–industry collaboration for entrepreneurship and innovation ecosystems 
Deepen South–South cooperation while reinforcing strategic South–North collaboration 
Convert research into implementation through pilot projects and scalable initiatives 
Expand academic mobility, joint programming, and collaborative research platforms 
Institutionalize research–policy interfaces that support diplomacy and international cooperation 
Nature of the Event
The Week integrates three complementary components:
1. GCUB General AssemblyA strategic forum for presidents, rectors, and institutional leaders focused on governance, planning, mobility, and international cooperation.
2. Multisectoral International ConferenceA platform for researchers, private-sector actors, Indigenous knowledge holders, policymakers, and artists. Academic papers will be selected through peer review, while other contributions will be curated through an expert-led process.
3. Kigali Knowledge Crossroads PlatformA broader academic-policy-societal forum featuring:
thematic panels and workshops 
policy dialogues and university-government roundtables 
education reform sessions 
entrepreneurship and innovation forums 
partnership meetings and MoU signings 
youth and early-career engagement 
exhibitions and cultural programming 
Expected Outputs
policy briefs and advisory notes 
partnership agreements and MoUs 
joint programs and mobility schemes 
pilot projects and innovation initiatives 
collaborative research agendas 
Scale and Participants
The forum will convene approximately 400–500 participants from five continents, including university leaders, researchers, students, government representatives, diplomats, entrepreneurs, civil society leaders, artists, innovators, and development partners. This diversity ensures that knowledge producers, decision-makers, and societal actors collaborate to co-design and implement partnerships.

Strategic Implementation Outcomes
Knowledge in Action Week is designed to generate concrete, measurable, and durable outcomes that extend well beyond the event itself. The Week will serve as a launchpad for year-long and multi-year collaborations facilitated by the Africana Bridging Center in partnership with participating institutions.
1.Academic Mobility and Access
MoUs, joint supervision models, tuition cooperation, and pilot mobility schemes in priority disciplines.
2. Joint Research and Knowledge Production
Thematic research consortia, collaborative projects, co-authored publications, and applied research outputs.
3. Curriculum and Pedagogical Innovation
Co-developed curricula aligned with societal needs, experiential learning models, and shared pilot programs.
4. Policy Engagement and Decision Support
Policy briefs, structured university–government dialogue, and research-to-policy pathways.
5.University–Industry and Innovation Partnerships
Innovation platforms, startup support, incubation initiatives, and investment-ready research projects.
6.Coordination, Evaluation, and Impact 
Ongoing coordination support and voluntary progress-sharing moments, with additional agreements and collaborations emerging after the Kigali Week, facilitated by the Africana Bridging Center.
ABOUT US
The Global Platform for the South (GPS) is a transdisciplinary ecosystem and convening platform dedicated to advancing inclusive development, innovation, and social transformation across the Global South. Headquartered in Kigali, with regional hubs in Addis Ababa, Dakar, and São Paulo, GPS connects institutions, decision-makers, practitioners, and collaborative networks to generate context-responsive solutions to shared challenges. Over the next two years, the platform aims to establish a presence in every major region of the Global South and the Global North through strategic partnerships, hubs, and affiliated universities, institutes, and centers.
GPS serves as a bridge between ideas and implementation, transforming vision into research, research into knowledge, knowledge into policy and partnerships, and partnerships into action that generates results, improves lives, and strengthens societies. It advances sustainable and locally grounded progress through collaboration across education, innovation, entrepreneurship, and community leadership.
It is designed as a platform where the South not only participates in global conversations, but also helps co-shape them.
The name GPS reflects both direction and impact.
Direction: Just as a global positioning system helps people locate themselves and navigate complex terrains, GPS enables actors across the Global South to identify opportunities, align priorities, and chart pathways toward shared futures. It represents clarity of purpose, strategic coordination, and meaningful connection across regions. 
Impact: GPS is equally committed to ensuring that ideas lead to action and that knowledge produces measurable change. The platform is built to transform vision into research, research into knowledge, knowledge into policy and partnerships, and partnerships into action that generates results, improves lives, and strengthens societies. 
Our guiding framework: KPI — Knowledge, Policy, and Impact.
KPI expresses the operating philosophy of the platform: that knowledge must inform policy, and policy must generate local and real-world impact. It reflects a commitment to evidence-based decision-making, practical collaboration, and continuous transformation across education, governance, innovation, and community development.
