Global Platform for the South (GPS)
FOUNDATIONS
Context & Rationale 
*GPS is a transdisciplinary research and policy ecosystem and platform
The Global Platform for the South (GPS) is a transdisciplinary research ecosystem,  policy platform, and convening institution dedicated to advancing applied knowledge and innovation for social transformation across the Global South. Rooted in the South and globally connected, the GPS brings together universities, governments, communities, civil society actors, strategic partners, entrepreneurs, and artists to co-design and implement context-responsive solutions to shared challenges.
The GPS aims at connecting and mobilizing the abundant sources of oral knowledge, indigenous experience, and inherent creative and scientific innovation that lack visibility and lay in inertia across Africa, Latin America and the Caribbean, Asia and the Middle East, and their diasporas.
The GPS intends to connect and mobilize the abundant sources of oral knowledge, indigenous experience, and inherent creative and scientific innovation that lay in inertia across Africa, Latin America and the Caribbean, Asia and the Middle East, and their diasporas, waiting for visibility.
This dynamic aims to ensure the full translation and localization of our innovative ideas into concrete policies, actionable solutions, and measurable impact.
This Southern reality unfolds within a rich history of vision and collective action from the Bandung Conference to the Lagos Plan of Action, the Abuja Treaty, the ASEAN Community Vision, Arab regional frameworks, the Buenos Aires Plan of Action, CELAC initiatives, CARICOM integration efforts, and the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. The Global South has consistently advanced ambitious foundational frameworks for sovereignty, regional integration, cooperation, and long-term development.
However, a gap persists between vision and implementation, and numerous structural constraints shape this dynamic across several Southern state contexts. They include:
fragmentation across sectors such as governance, economy, technology, environment, and education
limited alignment between knowledge production and public policy
under-integration of local, indigenous, and experiential knowledge systems
challenges to scaling research and innovation into sustainable, system-wide impact
misaligned incentive systems across academia, policy, and funding environments
Meanwhile, deeply interconnected systemic challenges interface an increasingly complex landscape shaped by climate change, inequality, digital transformation, and geopolitical shifts, and conflicts in the Global South..
They compartmentalize knowledge production within specialized or isolated domains without sustained dialogue across sectors or everyday realities. Several other interrelated factors, the enduring influence of colonial legacies and forms of epistemic eurocentrism continue to shape research agendas, academic hierarchies, and the valuation of knowledge systems, limiting the integration of locally grounded perspectives to regional priorities.
Public policy processes also do not draw on the full range of home expertise, and closely tie decision-making to externally driven technical assistance frameworks and sources of development financing—which constrain the space for locally produced knowledge and policy.
Consequently, the academic landscape faces internal tensions, research trajectories orient critique  for lacking pathways to application, or they align with dominant global paradigms displaying insufficient contextual adaptation. These tendencies weaken the translation of local knowledge into scalable impact.
Many contexts show underdeveloped institutional interfaces between research and policy—creating a “missing middle” that limits the systematic translation of knowledge into decision-making.
A knowledge ecosystem, by contrast, requires structured collaboration across disciplines, regions, and sectors—especially with policymakers—aiming to better inform public decisions in such areas as public health, education, and environmental policy. GPS rebuilds these connections—linking knowledge, policy, and practice across different fields and contexts, and helps in moving ideas from research into real-world solutions. It bridges this “missing middle” by creating pathways between ideas and implementation. It calls for equitable partnerships, aligning locally grounded research to priorities defined by the Global South states.
These realities do not simply require new plans, but a renewed approach for framing, connecting, mobilizing, and translating Southern knowledge into action research. At its core, GPS recognizes the link between sustainable development and the continuous circulation and application of knowledge across institutions, communities, and decision-making spaces—linking ideas to policy, and policy to action.
Thus, GPS emerges as a next-generation platform placing academic, local, indigenous, and experiential knowledge at the center, and organising action across interconnected domains—methodology, epistemology, culture, socio-economic systems, technology, and environment. GPS enables  a living ecosystem and integrated functions across sectors that have long operated in isolation.
GPS’ core framework—Knowledge → Policy → Impact (KPI)— serves as a diagnostic platform for identifying and understanding key challenges and gaps. It functions as a policy interface, connects knowledge to decision-making processes, and informing policy design. It also acts as an implementation bridge by supporting the translation of ideas and policies into concrete action and measurable impact.
Headquartered in Kigali, with regional hubs across the Global South and partnerships in the Global North, GPS functions as a platform of plural knowledge systems where institutions and communities, civil society actors, strategic partners, entrepreneurs, artists and governments work together to generate scalable, context-responsive solutions.
About GPS 
The name Global Platform for the South (GPS) focuses on identity and function, as “form follows function” (Louis Sullivan). It reflects both identity and functional agency as:
Global: interconnecting solution research to challenges across regions, disciplines, and systems
Platform: enabling coordination, collaboration, and collective action
South: fostering a space  sharing history, experience, creativity, and knowledge—including its diasporas
Like a navigation system, GPS helps actors:
locate themselves within evolving global knowledge systems
align priorities across institutions and regions
chart pathways toward shared futures
In honoring the spirit of navigation, GPS also recognizes Gladys West, whose pioneering work contributed to modern satellite positioning—reminding us that transformative knowledge often emerges from communities and places that are too often overlooked.
At its core, GPS is a living platform powered by people, institutions, and ideas in motion. Inspired by the Sankofa principle, GPS affirms that the future is shaped by drawing on the wisdom of the past in the present actions. Sankofa and GPS connect traditions—indigenous and endogenous knowledge systems, lived experiences, and local realities—with emerging technologies and global perspectives.
GPS is simultaneously:
a hub of research and knowledge institutions
a policy implementation platform
a leadership and training ecosystem
an innovating and accelerating partnership pivot 
a global convening forum for dialogue
a strategic catalyst for Global South transformation
Together, these dimensions position GPS not merely as a forum for dialogue, but as an integrated ecosystem designed to connect knowledge, policy, innovation, and action in ways that generate measurable and sustainable impact.
Vision
GPS envisions a future where the intellectual, cultural, scientific, creative, and entrepreneurial forces of the Global South are deeply connected to the socio-economic challenges and aspirations of their societies, and where collaboration across disciplines, sectors, cultures, and knowledge systems contributes to a more sustainable and inclusive world.
Mission
GPS advances socially grounded and evidence-based transformation by integrating scientific research, indigenous knowledge systems, innovation ecosystems, artistic practice, economic development, and policy design. Its mission is to connect science and policy, communities and institutions, and research and action in order to foster informed decision-making, inclusive collaboration, and measurable societal impact across the Global South and beyond.

Governance & Organizational Structure
1. Board of Directors 
The GPS Board of Directors provide institutional oversight, governance, fiduciary responsibility, and long-term strategic direction.
2. International Advisory Board (IAB)
The IAB provides strategic global guidance, strengthens institutional credibility and international visibility, supports partnerships and resource mobilization, and helps position GPS within global and Global South ecosystems of knowledge, policy, innovation, and development.
It comprises a distinguished and internationally recognized body of university leaders, current and former policymakers, ministers, diplomats, private sector leaders, donor representatives, and leading scholars and experts, reflecting the diversity, priorities, and aspirations of the Global South and its partners worldwide. The Board serves as a platform for bridge-building across academia, policy, diplomacy, philanthropy, business, and innovation ecosystems, advancing GPS’s mission of translating knowledge into policy, partnerships, and measurable impact.
