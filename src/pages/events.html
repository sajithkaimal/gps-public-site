Boubacar Boris Diop, Writer and GPS Associate Director, Honored at an International Colloquium in Dakar

Join scholars, writers, and readers in celebrating Boubacar Boris Diop’s work at Cheikh Anta Diop University. The colloquium is also accessible online,via YouTube online stream and sessions on Microsoft Teams.

Boubacar Boris Diop writer, poet, journalist, essayist, and Associate Director of the Global Platform for the South (GPS), will be honored at an international colloquium at Cheikh Anta Diop University in Dakar, Senegal, from October 26–28, 2026.Bringing together scholars, writers, and journalists from institutions across several continents, the gathering will explore his contributions to literature, historical memory, African languages, translation, and public debate. The program includes lectures by Felwine Sarr and Mohamed Mbougar Sarr, scholarly discussions, and artistic performances, culminating in a celebration of Diop’s 80th birthday.

GPS warmly welcomes this tribute to an intellectual whose work connects knowledge, cultural expression, and social responsibility. His writing on the Genocide against the Tutsi in Rwanda, his advocacy for Wolof and other African languages, and his engagement with writers and thinkers across regions resonate deeply with our mission. Diop brings critical, artistic, and cultural perspectives to understanding the challenges facing the Global South and imagining responses grounded in its peoples’ experiences. His contributions enrich GPS’s efforts to connect universities, governments, businesses, artists, and communities, creating opportunities for research, creativity, technology, and policy to inform one another and generate practical solutions. We congratulate our colleague and thank the organizers and participants for honoring his work and opening new conversations about its enduring relevance.

A new chapter in South–South cooperation

GPS and UMST-Rwanda Sign MoU to Strengthen Higher Education, Research and South–South Cooperation

The Global Platform for the South (GPS) and the University of Medical Sciences and Technology (UMST-Rwanda) have signed a Memorandum of Understanding, establishing a framework for strategic cooperation in higher education, research, innovation, academic mobility and knowledge exchange.

Higher educationResearchInnovation
Read moreRead less↓

The agreement was signed by Prof. Jean-Pierre Karegeye, Executive Director of GPS, and Prof. Samir Shaheen, Vice Chancellor of UMST-Rwanda. It provides a foundation for developing practical programs and partnerships connecting UMST with institutions across the wider GPS network.

Reflecting on the agreement’s significance, Prof. Samir Shaheen emphasized its role in translating solidarity into academic cooperation:

“This partnership with the Global Platform for the South is significant because it turns our shared commitment to South–South solidarity into a structured framework for academic mobility, joint research, teaching, innovation, and knowledge exchange,”

Highlighting the partnership’s potential to strengthen institutional collaboration and translate intangible knowledge into tangible benefits for communities across the Global South, Prof. Jean-Pierre Karegeye stated:

“This partnership reflects GPS’s commitment to connecting institutions across the Global South and translating knowledge into practice and action. UMST brings valuable expertise in education, healthcare and community service to this shared mission”

Resilience, African Solidarity and Internationalization

Founded in Khartoum, Sudan, by Prof. Mamoun Homieda in 1996, UMST began with two faculties and 60 students. By 2021, it had grown to 17 faculties, 27 postgraduate degrees and more than 8,000 students. Following the outbreak of war in Sudan, the university established a campus in Kigali.

Prof. Shaheen recalls and appreciates how Rwanda’s warm welcome and institutional cooperation helped sustain academic life at UMST through the establishment of its Rwandan campus in 2023:

“UMST’s experience in Rwanda is central to that story. After the conflict in Sudan disrupted our operations in Khartoum in 2023, Rwanda welcomed us. Through “GO AFRICA” and in collaboration with Rwandan institutions, including the University of Rwanda, we kept students and academics learning, teaching, and researching,”

This experience demonstrates how cooperation among African states and institutions can preserve educational opportunities during crises. Rwanda has welcomed approximately 5,000 Sudanese students, most of whom are enrolled at UMST, illustrating the importance of regional solidarity in safeguarding knowledge and nurturing talent. The UMST-Rwanda has not only graduated a large number of Sudanese, Rwandan and other African and international students, it has also expanded its operations through establishing Kabutare and Nyanza hospitals and Frontier Diagnostic Center, adding to country’s talent pool and economic viability.

Education Connected to Healthcare and Community Life

UMST combines academic training with practical engagement in Rwanda’s healthcare system. Its clinical partnerships connect professional education with services for local patients, while participation in community activities brings students and staff into closer contact with the people they serve.

Describing these contributions, Prof. Samir Shaheen highlighted the relationship between Pan-African solidarity, healthcare and public service:

“Today, our presence in Rwanda is a practical expression of Pan-Africanism: we not only educate professionals but also work within Rwanda’s healthcare system, supporting dental services at Kabutare and Nyanza hospitals. Frontier Diagnostic Center is a state-of-the-art facility providing quality health services and day-case medical care. It is well equipped and affiliated with UMST as a sister institution. We also participate in Umuganda with our communities,”

The university’s support for dental services includes equipment, expertise and training. Its participation in Umuganda, Rwanda’s collective community service initiative, further reflects an approach to education that connects professional preparation with social responsibility.

UMST’s mission emphasizes preparing competent, socially responsible professionals through academic programs that respond to local, national and global needs. This commitment provides a strong foundation for its cooperation with GPS.

Priorities of the Partnership

The GPS-UMST MoU identifies several complementary areas of cooperation:

  • Academic mobility and talent development: student and staff exchanges, visiting scholars, research fellowships, postgraduate mobility and joint research supervision.
  • Joint research and knowledge exchange: collaborative and transdisciplinary research, publications, policy briefs, research networks, and the sharing of indigenous and endogenous knowledge.
  • Teaching and capacity development: short courses, professional training, executive education, workshops, and online, blended, or hybrid learning.
  • Innovation and entrepreneurship: university–industry collaboration, technology transfer, incubation, and connections between researchers, innovators, investors and markets.
  • Funding and institutional partnerships: joint funding proposals and multinational, multidisciplinary consortia.
  • Young researchers and inclusion: mentorship, leadership development and expanded international academic opportunities, particularly for underrepresented groups.
  • Knowledge dissemination and dialogue: multilingual resources, digital repositories, conferences, webinars and policy forums.

Implementation of the MoU may be supported through annual or biennial action plans and designated institutional focal points. Specific programs will be developed through mutual agreement, taking account of available resources and relevant academic approvals.

As part of this partnership, UMST will lead GPS’s Inclusive Health Department, contributing its academic expertise, clinical experience, and commitment to community service. Looking ahead to this role, Prof. Samir Shaheen outlined the university’s broader ambitions within the Global Platform for the South:

“For UMST, South–South cooperation means connecting African expertise with institutions and partners across Asia, Latin America, the Caribbean, and the Middle East, and turning knowledge into practical action. Through GPS and by leading its Inclusive Health Department, we aim to build concrete programs that benefit students, researchers, institutions, and the communities they serve, because when the Global South cooperates, resilience becomes opportunity and solidarity becomes development,”

Connecting Universities Across the Global South

GPS has also facilitated cooperation between UMST and the International Cooperation Group of Brazilian Universities (GCUB), a network of 104 universities. This engagement led to the signing of a second MoU establishing a framework for academic exchange, joint research and knowledge sharing between African and Brazilian institutions.

Headquartered in Kigali, GPS connects universities, governments, industry, civil society and communities through regional hubs across Africa, Latin America, the Caribbean, the Middle East and Asia. It is also exploring cooperation with the Global South Think Tanks Alliance in China.

The partnership with UMST brings an important dimension to this network: the experience of an African university that has sustained its educational mission through displacement while contributing to its host country’s healthcare and community life. Together, the two institutions seek to build on that experience through academic collaboration, inclusive health initiatives and opportunities for students and researchers across regions.
