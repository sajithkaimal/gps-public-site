Assessment
What's Clear and Strong
Vision is well-articulated. GPS has a clear operating philosophy (KPI: Knowledge -> Policy -> Impact) and a detailed five-year roadmap with yearly milestones. The thematic depth is impressive - epistemic pluralism, indigenous knowledge, and South-South cooperation are not buzzwords here; they are woven into the program architecture.
Governance structure is designed thoughtfully. A small Board + International Advisory Board + Regional Hubs + Division Chiefs model is appropriate for a transnational platform at this stage.
Kigali 2027 is the critical milestone. Everything in 2026 feeds into this event. It is the organization's proof-of-concept moment - 400-500 participants from five continents, tied to GCUB. If this lands well, credibility compounds. If it does not, the platform struggles to gain traction.
Adu O.B. brings complementary expertise. His focus on data analytics, impact measurement, and policy engagement pairs well with the CTO's digital/product mandate. The Sajith-Adu duo could be a powerful operating core.
What Needs Attention Now
1.  Legal entity and fiscal structure do not exist yet. The roadmap says legal structures are a Year 1 activity, but this is a blocker for hiring, bank accounts, contracts, and grant applications. No tech work matters if GPS cannot legally receive funds or employ people.
2.  The tech vision is aspirational, not operational. GPS Digital Launch, GPS Digital Knowledge Ecosystem, Global South Data & Foresight Platform, and GPS Academy are mentioned, but no architecture, stack, or budget is defined. The CTO needs to move from 'we will build digital tools' to 'here is what we are building first, how, and what it costs.'
3.  The Advisory Board is not yet committed. Many IAB members are under discussion, TBC, or contact under discussion via a named person. This is normal for a pre-launch organization, but it means GPS's credibility anchor is still forming. The CTO should not depend on IAB input for initial tech decisions.
4.  Resource mobilization has no numbers. The roadmap mentions fundraising strategy, donor engagement framework, and endowment vision, but no targets, timelines, or pipelines. Without revenue assumptions, tech hiring and infrastructure planning is guesswork.
5.  The missing-middle problem is real, and GPS is solving it - but the solution is expensive. Bridging research and policy requires sustained engagement, not one-off events. The Innovation Labs, Policy Advisory Programs, and Community-Based Implementation initiatives all need ongoing staffing and funding, not just conference budgets.

Recommendations
1.  Scope tech work to 2026 priorities only. Year 1 activities are: digital launch (website + identity + social media), GPS Dialogues Series (virtual conversations), and Kigali 2027 prep. Everything else - GPS Academy, Accelerator, Data Platform, Impact Observatory - is Year 2+. Push those back.
2.  Build a minimal viable platform for GPS Digital Launch. A clean website, social media presence, and a simple knowledge-sharing portal are enough for the first step. Do not build a complex ecosystem before there is content and funding.
3.  Propose a phased tech plan to the Board. Use Phase 1 for the website, social media, Kigali 2027 event landing page, newsletter, and blog. Use Phase 2 for GPS Academy, GPS Outlook, and GPS Journal. Use Phase 3 for the Accelerator portal, Data & Foresight Platform, and Impact Observatory.
4.  Clarify the CTO scope. The intro email suggests a technology leadership role with product strategy, digital platforms, and market development. But the org documents list Technology & Innovation as one of six domains. Confirm whether the CTO leads only that domain or has organization-wide authority over technology, product, data, and platform decisions.
5.  Push for a governance and budget document. Before committing to any tech roadmap, GPS needs legal entity details, 2026 budget, confirmed team headcount, and Board approval authority for tech spending.
